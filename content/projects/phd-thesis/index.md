My PhD thesis in physics. It is about materials one atom thick, mainly graphene and hexagonal boron nitride, used as membranes, with desalination, energy harvesting and DNA sensing in view.

It opens with a review of ion transport and molecular sensing in two-dimensional materials, and of the ways to make pores in them. The experiments use a nanocapillary as the platform. They show that graphene and boron nitride let positive ions through in preference to negative ones, and look at why. Control of the surface charge then makes the membranes favour negative ions instead. Last comes control of the pores themselves, shrinking and growing them, and their use to detect strands of DNA passing through.

The final part turns to quantum dots, which can report a voltage through the light they give off. A platform is built to calibrate that response in an electric field, and the dots are then measured in the membranes of neurons.
