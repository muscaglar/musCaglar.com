I want everything, ideally, to be at room temperature (about 25 to 30 °C) before combining.

## Sabayon

1. In a metal bowl, combine the egg yolks, the 40 g of sugar and the rum. Whisk until combined and beginning to lighten in colour.
2. Place the bowl over a pan of low simmering water.
3. Continue whisking on high for around 8 to 10 min.
4. When the temperature hits 75 °C, take it off the heat.
5. Plunge the bowl into cold tap water or ice water. Cool to approximately 28 to 30 °C: warm enough that it folds smoothly into the mascarpone, but not hot enough to melt anything.
6. Do not scrape down the sides too aggressively. There might be cooked yolk.

## Egg whites

1. Add 1 tsp of lemon juice to a clean mixing bowl.
2. Add the egg whites.
3. Whisk on medium for 1 min until bubbly. If the whites are cold it will take a minute longer.
4. Whisk on high for 3 to 4 min, gradually adding the 12 g of sugar.
5. Stop at a stiff peak, just as it begins to stiffen and hold its shape. Don't overdo it here.

## Whipped cream

1. Whisk the cream with the 12 g of sugar until it is just about to set and hold its shape.
2. Set aside. Do not add the mascarpone here. It goes in separately.

## Combination

1. Lightly beat the room-temperature mascarpone until smooth and loose. Add the zest and mix in.
2. Fold the cooled sabayon into the mascarpone in 2 to 3 additions until fully combined. These are the two densest, most stable elements. Combining them first creates a uniform base.
3. Fold in the first third of the egg whites somewhat vigorously. This sacrificial addition loosens the dense base so that the remaining whites don't deflate on contact.
4. Fold in the second and third additions gently: spatula down the centre, scoop under, fold over. Stop as soon as no white streaks remain.
5. Gently fold in the whipped cream in 2 additions. Some deflation is normal, maybe 10 to 20% of the volume. That's fine.

## Soak

1. Heat 350 ml of water to 80 to 90 °C (let a boiled kettle sit for a minute). Mix in the 50 g of sugar until dissolved.
2. Add the hojicha powder and whisk until combined.
3. Let the soak cool to room temperature before dipping.
4. Dip each ladyfinger for 1 to 2 seconds per side. They should look damp but still feel sturdy. The biscuits go on absorbing moisture from the cream layers in the fridge, so they should feel slightly firm at assembly.
5. The soak can be prepared in 2 to 3 batches to keep the concentration consistent.

## Assembly

1. Layer the soaked biscuits and the cream in the dish.
2. Refrigerate for at least 6 h, ideally overnight.
3. Dust with hojicha powder before serving.

## Notes

My notes hold two versions of this recipe. This page follows the second, which is the fuller. In the first the mascarpone goes into the cream and the soak is kept hot. The choices between the two are still open.
